# Independent A/B review

Use the standalone reviewer packet. Each reviewer receives an identical blank copy of the worksheet and the original English, Hindi references, and randomized A/B outputs. The 30 items, their order, and the A/B assignments are frozen. Use the field-level rubric included in the packet.

1. Recruit two people proficient in English and Hindi who did not build or score the models. Each completes their own worksheet without discussing items with the other reviewer. They may use the reference alternatives, but the English controls in the event of an inconsistent reference.
2. For each output score main action, participants and objects, quantity and referent, negation, and overall factual adequacy. Use 1, 0, NA or U as defined in the rubric. Give a short reason for failures, unclear Hindi, and reference disagreements. Judge each output on its own before comparing it with the other.
3. The coordinator checks all 60 overall fields and archives both raw worksheets with hashes before looking at model identity. Record agreement and disagreement per criterion, excluding NA from its denominator. Record how many overall judgments remain unresolvable.
4. A third qualified bilingual adjudicator receives only the blinded disputed items, the rubric, and the two anonymous rationales. They enter a final score or an unresolved flag. Archive the original ratings as well as the adjudicated ratings and reasons. If a third reviewer is unavailable, keep disagreement unresolved and leave the gate pending.
5. Freeze the adjudicated file and only then unlock the separate arm key. Compute paired wins, losses and ties on overall adequacy. Pilot 3 required six net treatment wins in 30. Report the outcome separately from the Pilot 4 diagnostic. The reviewer's role is to score the outputs, regardless of whether Pilot 3 met the automatic metrics.
